# NVDA: How exposed is NVIDIA to US export controls on China, and how is that showing up in results?

*As of 2026-09-30. Research output, not investment advice.*

## Bull case

- Despite US export restrictions, NVIDIA achieved total revenue of $215,938,000,000 for the fiscal year ending January 25, 2026, delivering 65.5% revenue growth year-over-year. [^13][^16][^3]
- NVIDIA maintained strong profitability throughout export control changes, generating gross profit of $153,463,000,000 with a 71.1% gross margin and net income of $120,067,000,000 for the fiscal year ending January 25, 2026. [^11][^12][^14][^15]
- Growth in non-China markets has significantly outpaced China, with United States revenue reaching $149,617 million and Taiwan revenue reaching $42,345 million in the fiscal year ended January 25, 2026, compared to $19,677 million from China (including Hong Kong). [^3]
- In the three months ended July 26, 2026, NVIDIA generated $96,221 million in total revenue, supported by $60,074 million from the United States and $26,985 million from Taiwan. [^10]
- NVIDIA continues to navigate licensing restrictions while driving platform expansion, securing licenses in August 2025 to ship certain H20 products to certain China-based customers while continuing its Blackwell GPU revenue ramp. [^8][^7]

## Bear case

- US export licensing requirements imposed on NVIDIA's H20 integrated circuits forced the company to incur a $4.5 billion charge in the first quarter of fiscal year 2026 associated with excess inventory and purchase obligations as demand for H20 products diminished. [^5][^8][^7]
- Export controls have hurt full-year results in China, with annual geographic revenue from China (including Hong Kong) falling from $25,048 million in the fiscal year ended Jan 26, 2025 to $19,677 million in the fiscal year ended Jan 25, 2026. [^3]
- Top-line declines in China continued into fiscal year 2027, with quarterly revenue from China (including Hong Kong) dropping from $9,659 million in the three months ended Apr 27, 2025 to $4,550 million in the three months ended Apr 26, 2026, while China Data Center revenue as a percentage of total Data Center revenue remains well below levels seen prior to export controls in October 2023. [^9][^1]
- NVIDIA faces compounding commercial pressure as U.S. restrictions cover extensive product families—such as A100, A800, H100, H800, L4, L40, L40S, RTX 4090, GB200 NVL72, B200, and H20—while the Chinese government actively encourages customers to buy from domestic competitors and restricts or discourages purchases of NVIDIA's data center products, including China-specific offerings designed to comply with U.S. export controls. [^4][^6][^2]
